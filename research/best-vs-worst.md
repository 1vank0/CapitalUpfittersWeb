# Best vs. Worst

Head-to-head contrast of the strongest and weakest patterns found across all 50 researched sites, organized by what the redesign should copy vs. avoid. Evidence type noted; "observed" = read directly from fetched page content, "inferred" = triangulated from indirect evidence (see `research-evidence.json` for per-site detail).

## Trust and proof

**Best — Leonard USA:** "4.8/5 from 3,818 reviews" — specific, large, recent-window ("past 12 months"). Specificity is what makes it credible.
**Worst — Coastal Linings:** zero reviews, certifications, or years-in-business visible anywhere. **Worst pattern — Baltimore Bedliners / APS Johnstown:** inherited franchisor claims ("25 years," "over 30 years between founders and 150+ years inside our board members") presented as the shop's own history rather than the parent brand's — this reads as borrowed authority, not earned trust, and is the exact trap Capital Upfitters (also a Patriot Liner licensee) must avoid. Directly relevant to `project-memory/FACTS-TO-VERIFY.md` — none of Capital's own tenure/experience claims go live without independent verification.

## Lead generation / conversion path

**Best — Leonard USA:** "Get My Free Quote," no deposit required, explicit "responds within 1 business day" promise — removes the two biggest quote-request objections (cost, uncertainty) in the CTA copy itself.
**Worst — Alliance Fleet:** a real Mercedes-Benz Master Upfitter credential, wasted behind a footer-only "Contact Us" link with no visible form. Strong credibility, self-sabotaged conversion path.

## Positioning and differentiation

**Best — Truck'n America / Ted Britt Truck Shop:** both claim a specific, ownable identity (50-year family-owned; Ford-certified fleet specialist) rather than generic "quality service" language.
**Worst — Baltimore Bedliners / APS Johnstown:** identical templated positioning copy across two unrelated cities confirms the Patriot Liner network's default is genericness. This is the single most important comparison for Capital Upfitters specifically, since it shares the same franchise relationship.

## Service discovery

**Best — Truck'n America / APS Rust & Tint:** broad catalogs presented with clear categorical structure (caps, tonneau, bedliners, accessories, trailers // undercoating, tint, PPF, ceramic, detailing, fleet).
**Worst — Alliance Fleet:** broad real capability, presented as undifferentiated prose blocks with no clear service navigation.

## Fleet/commercial experience

**Best — Holman / Advantage Outfitters:** purpose-built fleet flows — dedicated fleet-size/role intake fields, OEM-authorized upfitter badges stacked visibly, EV-fleet-specific content.
**Worst — Capital Wrappers / Leonard USA:** fleet is an afterthought, not a distinct journey, despite otherwise strong retail-side execution — a reminder that Capital Upfitters' redesign needs genuinely separate retail vs. fleet/government journeys (per `project-memory/REQUIREMENTS.md`), not one generic form serving both.

## Content and topical authority

**Best — Capital Wrappers:** extensive DMV-specific geo-landing pages plus blog content addressing MD/DC tint-law specifics — directly useful, locally-relevant education, not generic filler.
**Worst — APS Johnstown / Baltimore Bedliners:** thin, templated content with no local specificity beyond a swapped city name.

## What this means for `best-principles.md`
The recurring worst-pattern across this research is inherited/generic templating — exactly the risk Capital Upfitters faces as a Patriot Liner licensee. The recurring best-pattern is specific, verifiable, locally-grounded proof paired with a low-friction, audience-segmented quote path. Both directly inform the trust framework and journey design in `best-principles.md`.
